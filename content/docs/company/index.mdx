Kovati builds the software layer that turns generated content into content a
game can actually use.

That is a narrower claim than it sounds, and the narrowness is the point.
There is no shortage of tools that produce an animation, a voice take, a face
solve or a mesh. There is a severe shortage of anything that gets that result
onto the right skeleton, into the right montage slot, carrying the right
gameplay event, bound to the right ability, with a record of what made it and
a safe way to replace it later.

We call that distance **the playable gap**, and closing it **PlayableOps**.

## Why a game studio built a tools company

Automation Forge is built inside **Colony Origins**, a real Unreal Engine
production, and nothing ships from it that has not first been used there.

This is not a marketing arrangement. It is the only quality gate we trust. A
plugin written against an imagined project passes every test its author
thought of; a plugin used to actually finish a scene fails in the specific,
unglamorous ways that matter — the skeleton faces the wrong axis, the prop
leaves the hand, the face montage plays on the body mesh, the NPC cached its
bark at spawn and never heard the new one.

Every hard-won fact in this wiki came from that loop. Where a page tells you
something oddly specific, it is because the general version was wrong once.

## The three product lines

**Automation Forge** is the first and by far the largest — a family of Unreal
Engine plugins covering motion, speech, face, mesh and performance, with a hub
that installs them and pipelines that run them unattended. That is what
[most of this wiki](/docs/automation-forge) is about.

**Kovati Studio** is the video production workspace we use to record and cut
tutorials and demos. It is not released — the desktop app and the Windows
companion both ship before it is documented here.

**Kovati Pro** is the shared, web-hosted version of the pipelines: the same
definitions and the same ledger, operated for a team rather than on one
workstation. It does not exist yet. The
[whitepaper](https://kovati.dev/whitepaper) describes what it is meant to be.

<Callout type="info">
  Two of those three have no documentation here, deliberately. A section that
  promises documentation and delivers a placeholder is worse than no section

</Callout>
