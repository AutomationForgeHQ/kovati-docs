From nothing installed to a landscape in a new map. The [Automation Forge
getting started](/docs/automation-forge/getting-started) covers the hub and the
account; this page starts after that.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Install through the hub

In the hub's **Plugins** tab, pick **TerrainForge** and press **Add &
install**. The open toolset installs in the same click. TerrainForge itself is
a paid plugin and is named until it is on your account; while it is in
preview, its checkout costs nothing.

```powershell
forge install terrainforge
```

In Unreal, open the **Automation Forge** toolbar menu, tick **TerrainForge**
under **Installed** (and **TerrainForgeToolset** if an agent will drive it),
and restart the editor. **Edit → Plugins** works too.

</Step>

<Step>

### Create a Terrain Definition from a preset

In the Content Browser: **Add → Automation Forge → TerrainForge → Terrain
Definition (from preset)**.

The picker lists every preset with a small hillshade of its output, drawn by
the native engine in the background and cached. Choose **Alpine valleys
(eroded)** for a 4 km eroded mountain range, or any of the classic ones, and
press **Create**. The asset opens in the TerrainForge editor.

**Terrain Definition**, without "from preset", opens with a starter mountain
graph instead.

On a definition you already have, **Start from preset** in the toolbar
replaces the graph and world settings in one undoable step. It keeps the
definition's embedded sources and clears its GIS provenance. It is disabled
while a build runs.

</Step>

<Step>

### Preview

Select a node in the **Flow graph** and the **Terrain viewer** shows it, with
**Live preview** on. Double-clicking a node previews it too.

- The preview grid is 129, 257, 513 or 1025 samples. *"Preview grid. Builds
  use the toolbar's resolution."* A 257 × 257 preview comes first, then the
  size you asked for.
- **Pin** keeps previewing one node while you select and edit the nodes that
  feed it.
- In drainage water mode, the preview of the graph output also draws its lakes
  and rivers.
- The viewer draws at most a set number of vertices per side; it says so under
  the view. A full 4033 mesh needs a lot of memory, so raise it on purpose.

Previews are quick looks on a reduced grid. A build computes the take at its
own resolution, so a graph that previews well at 513 is still worth checking
at 2017 before a 4033 build.

</Step>

<Step>

### Build a take

**World & biomes** holds the world every build uses: map size, output grid,
simulation grid, lowest and highest height, seed, which map edges water may
leave by, and the water mode. It shows an estimate of the build's peak memory
before you start.

Press **Build terrain**. The resolution beside it is **World settings**,
**Native 2K · 2017²** or **Native 4K · 4033²**. Start at 2017; 4033 is several
minutes on the CPU.

The **Build monitor** tab follows the build node by node and keeps the last
twelve builds of each definition. **Cancel** stops the running build; takes
that finished are kept.

The result is a new [take](/docs/automation-forge/terrainforge#takes). A
changed graph or world setting marks the last take out of date; the take
itself stays.

</Step>

<Step>

### Import it as a World Partition landscape

On the **Export** tab, under **New level**, give a content path that does not
exist yet (it starts as `/Game/TerrainForge/L_Terrain`) and press **Import as
new level**.

The importer creates a new World Partition map with:

- the landscape, at the take's resolution, with one paint layer per layer of
  the take, their weights summing to 255;
- a landscape material that paints each layer a flat colour, for inspection;
- the take's lakes and rivers as engine water bodies, their beds already
  carved;
- a directional light, sky light, sky atmosphere and height fog;
- a provenance record on the landscape, tagged `TerrainForge`.

It refuses, before it creates anything, when there is no current completed
take, the path exists, the open map has unsaved changes, or Play In Editor is
running.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Bigger takes

| Take | In the editor | Nanite |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 4033 | Yes, if the memory guard finds room | On |
| 8129 | Yes, if the memory guard finds room | Off, with a warning; enable it on the proxies afterwards |
| 16321 | No: the headless commandlet | Off |

The **memory guard** estimates an import's peak and refuses one that will not
fit in free memory, saying how much it needs and how much is free.

The commandlet has no resolution cap. It builds the landscape **one streaming
proxy at a time**, so a 16321 take peaks near 8 GB rather than the 45 GB a
whole import would need:

```powershell
& "<UE 5.8>\Engine\Binaries\Win64\UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe" "<project>.uproject" `
  -run=TerrainForgeImport `
  "-TakeDir=<absolute path to the take folder>" `
  "-Level=/Game/TerrainForge/L_Large" `
  -unattended -nop4 -AllowCommandletRendering
```

- Add `-VerifyLevel=/Game/<map>` with the same `-TakeDir` to check a saved map
  against its take at every vertex. It writes `<map>-verify.json` and exits 0
  only when heights, paint weights and bounds all match.
- Build Nanite afterwards in batches of at most 16 proxies:
  `-run=WorldPartitionBuilderCommandlet -Builder=TerrainForgeLandscapeBuilder -FirstProxy=0 -ProxyCount=16`,
  then the next 16, until the reported total.
- `-NoMemoryGuard` turns the guard off for the commandlet.

## Export without a level

The **Export** tab can also bind a take's files to Landscape layers, PCG
masks, textures and water geometry, and write them as assets without creating
a level. **Suggest bindings** binds biome layers, PCG masks and water files by
name; **Validate** checks them; **Export assets** creates a new Terrain Export
Bundle with the textures and layer infos it names. No level is switched.

Biome masks add up; PCG masks overlap. Weight-blend only a complete,
normalised group of layers.
