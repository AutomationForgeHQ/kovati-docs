The **GIS import** tab of the TerrainForge editor starts a terrain from a real
place. You choose a square on a map, **Discover** asks the data providers what
they hold for it, and **Import** brings the elevation and the masks you ticked
into the definition's graph.

All of it runs inside the editor. It needs internet access to the providers,
and nothing else: no account, no key, no other program.

## The map

- **Search.** Type a place for suggestions from an offline list of towns and
  places (GeoNames, CC BY 4.0), or paste coordinates in decimal or
  degrees-minutes-seconds, or an OpenStreetMap or Google Maps link. **Enter**
  asks a web search (Photon, with Nominatim as fallback, or a keyed service set
  in the map settings).
- **The square.** Drag it to move it and a handle to resize it;
  Alt+double-click centres it, **F** frames it. Its centre must be within 80°
  of the equator. Its side is **500 m to 50 km**.
- **Grid.** The samples per side the sources are read at: 257, 513, 1025,
  2017, 2049 or 4033. *"A finer grid does not improve the source's own
  resolution."*
- **1 m level size** snaps the square to 2016 or 4032 m on a 2017 or 4033
  grid, the shape a [level project](/docs/automation-forge/terrainforge/levels)
  uses.
- **Overlays** draw a 1 km grid, the landscape components and streaming
  proxies the import will create, the open level's World Partition cells
  (approximate), and the area each elevation source covers.
- **Relief shading** puts a hillshade over the basemap and reads the elevation
  under the cursor.
- **Quick 3D** shows the square in the Terrain viewer from 30 m elevation
  tiles, and follows the square as it moves. *"It is not the import."*

**Basemaps are for looking, not importing.** *"Basemap tiles are for display
only. They are never written into a take, a texture, provenance or an export;
only the sources you import carry licences into the project."* The default is
OpenTopoMap; OpenStreetMap and a relief layer need no key; Esri World Imagery,
MapTiler, Stadia, Thunderforest and Mapbox layers appear when you enter their
keys in **Editor Preferences → Plugins → TerrainForge Map**. Each layer shows
its own attribution on the map.

## Discover

**Discover datasets** asks the providers what they cover in the square. It
takes 10 to 40 seconds and imports nothing. **Use this area** discovers first
when the square has moved, then imports what is ticked.

### Elevation, ranked

Every source whose coverage area reaches the square is **probed** on a 48 × 48
grid, and listed with its resolution and the share of the square it covers.
They are ranked in this order:

1. sources that allow commercial use before any that do not;
2. national and global surveys before the last-resort web tiles;
3. finer resolution first;
4. more coverage first.

Rank 1 is marked **recommended**. Above the individual sources sits
**Elevation · best available**: every ranked source in that order, the finest
first, **each one filling the voids of the one before**, with the web tiles
last. Choose it unless you have a reason to force one source.

A source that fails is still listed, with the reason: the provider's error, or
*"No answer within 25 s."* A source whose area does not reach the square is
left out. **One failing provider never fails Discover.**

### Land cover and OpenStreetMap

Discover also reads the ESA WorldCover classes present in the square, with
each class's share, and counts the OpenStreetMap features of each dataset.

A dataset with no features is **unmapped**, not absent: *"Mapped observations;
incomplete OSM coverage is not evidence of absence."*

## Elevation sources

Read from the plugin's `Resources/GIS/sources.json`, in the order ties are
broken:

| Source | Covers | Resolution | Kind | Licence | Credit required |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IGN LiDAR HD MNT | Metropolitan France; national coverage is still being completed | 0.5 m | Bare earth, airborne LiDAR | Licence Ouverte 2.0 (Etalab) | Yes |
| IGN RGE ALTI | All of France; coarser photogrammetry, up to 5 m, in parts of the mountains | 1 m | Bare earth | Licence Ouverte 2.0 (Etalab) | Yes |
| swisstopo swissALTI3D | Switzerland and Liechtenstein; newest survey per 1 km tile | 0.5 m | Bare earth | swisstopo Open Government Data | Yes |
| USGS 3DEP | The United States: the lower 48, Alaska, the Aleutians, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam and the Northern Marianas, American Samoa | 1 m lidar where flown, about 10 m elsewhere | Bare earth | Public domain | No (good practice) |
| Kartverket DTM | Norway | 1 m | Bare earth | CC BY 4.0 | Yes |
| AHN DTM | The Netherlands; buildings and water are voids by design, filled from the next source | 0.5 m | Bare earth, LiDAR | CC0 1.0 | No (good practice) |
| Environment Agency LIDAR Composite DTM | England | 1 m | Bare earth, LiDAR | Open Government Licence v3.0 | Yes |
| Geobasis NRW DGM1 | North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany | 1 m | Bare earth, LiDAR | dl-de/zero-2.0 | No (good practice) |
| Copernicus DEM GLO-30 | Worldwide, land only | 30 m | **Surface**: includes forest canopy and buildings | Copernicus DEM licence | Yes |
| Terrain Tiles (Terrarium) | Worldwide, within 80° latitude; last resort | about 30 m | Mixed sources | Mixed open sources, listed in `terrain-attribution.txt` | Yes |

**Every listed source allows commercial use.**

<Callout type="warn">
  **Copernicus is not bare earth.** It is a surface model: forest canopy reads as
  raised ground, and a town as blocks. Where a national
  bare-earth survey covers the square, it ranks above Copernicus.
</Callout>

- **Datum accuracy.** Elevation and OpenStreetMap are aligned to about 1 m,
  and to about 2 m in Great Britain, where the national grid-shift files are
  not used. Masks can sit that far off the terrain.
- Where a service answers with zeros or an error outside its survey (Norway,
  England), those samples count as voids, not as sea level.
- A forced single source that does not cover the whole square says so:
  *"… cover only x % of the square. Choose "elevation" (best available) to
  fill the rest from other sources."*
- Voids left after every source are filled from the nearest measured sample,
  and the import notes how many: *"… void pixels (…) were filled from the
  nearest measured sample."*

## Land cover

**ESA WorldCover 2021**, 10 m, worldwide, CC BY 4.0 (credit required). Ten
classes, each importable as its own mask:

| Dataset id | Class |
|---|---|
| `worldcover:trees` | Tree cover |
| `worldcover:shrubland` | Shrubland |
| `worldcover:grassland` | Grassland |
| `worldcover:cropland` | Cropland |
| `worldcover:built` | Built-up |
| `worldcover:bare` | Bare / sparse vegetation |
| `worldcover:snow` | Snow and ice |
| `worldcover:water` | Permanent water bodies |
| `worldcover:wetland` | Herbaceous wetland and mangroves |
| `worldcover:moss` | Moss and lichen |

Each class becomes a soft 0 to 1 mask, interpolated onto the grid and stored
as an 8-bit image. It is a 10 m classification: a fine edge on the map is a
10 m step in the mask.

## OpenStreetMap features

Fetched through the Overpass API. Each dataset becomes a mask, and the
features themselves are kept as vectors.

| Dataset id | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `water` | Mapped water (lakes and rivers) |
| `lakes` | Mapped lakes and ponds |
| `rivers` | Mapped rivers and streams |
| `forest` | Mapped woodland |
| `grassland` | Mapped grassland and meadows |
| `wetland` | Mapped wetland |
| `glacier` | Mapped glaciers |
| `scree` | Mapped scree |
| `bare_rock` | Mapped bare rock |
| `shingle` | Mapped shingle |
| `cliffs` | Mapped cliffs (buffered lines) |
| `roads` | Mapped roads |
| `paths` | Mapped paths and tracks |
| `buildings` | Mapped buildings |

- **Lines are painted at a width.** Roads, paths, rivers and water use the
  feature's `width` tag when it is a sensible number. Otherwise roads are 6 m,
  paths 2 m, cliffs 6 m and the rest 4 m, and the import says so: *"Line masks
  use approximate width where OSM has no width tag; vectors preserve the
  original centreline."*
- **A river is never a lake.** A water area tagged as a river, stream, canal,
  ditch, drain or rapids counts as a river, in the masks, in the site check
  and in a level build.
- Masks are 0 or 255. **They do not carve the terrain by themselves**; your
  graph decides what they do.

## Import

Tick the datasets, then **Import ticked** (or **Use this area**). One switch
decides what happens to the definition:

| Replace graph and world settings | Result |
|---|---|
| **On** | The graph and world settings are replaced. The elevation arrives as an *Imported heightfield* node in metres, wired to the output, and water is off. The world's height range is set from the data with a metre to spare. |
| **Off** | The sources are **added** as nodes to the existing graph, which must have the same physical size as the square. Nothing is aligned automatically. |

Every mask arrives as an *Imported mask* node named `gis_<dataset>`, exported
as a named map `GIS_<dataset>` and left unwired. Without an elevation dataset,
a replacing import starts from a flat placeholder.

The import is **one undoable step**. If the definition changes while the import
runs, the result is not applied over your edits: the import fails, keeps the
downloaded project in `Saved/TerrainForge/gis-imports/`, and names the file.

### Resolutions

The grid sets how often the sources are sampled. With **Replace** on, it also
sets the take's resolution:

| GIS grid | Take built at |
|---|---|
| 257 | 505 |
| 513 | 1009 |
| 1025 | 2017 |
| 2017 | 2017 |
| 2049 | 4033 |
| 4033 | 4033 |

With Replace off, the take keeps the definition's own resolution.

### Limits

- At most **15 mask datasets** in one import: *"a graph holds 16 source
  files."*
- At most 16 sources and 96 MiB of embedded data in the definition, and a
  portable project of at most 144 MiB.
- **One import or level build at a time** across the editor.
- A 4033 import needs about 1.2 GB of free memory. **It stops rather than
  page** when more than 85 % of the computer's memory is in use.
- An import reports its progress on the GIS tab and in the build monitor;
  **Cancel import** stops it.

With the data downloaded, a 4033 import of an alpine square with two land-cover
and ten OpenStreetMap masks takes 2 to 3 seconds. The download usually takes
longer, and depends on the providers and your connection.

## What is cached

| What | Where | For how long |
|---|---|---|
| Discover results | Memory | 10 minutes per square |
| Site-check results | Memory | 15 minutes per square and profile |
| Web elevation tiles (Terrarium) | `Saved/TerrainForge/GIS/terrarium` | Until the cache limit (2 GiB by default); least recently used go first |
| Map tiles | `Saved/TerrainForge/MapTiles` | Up to the disk budget (1 GB by default) |
| Import records | `Saved/TerrainForge/gis-imports` | The newest 20, at most 30 days |

Elevation from the national services, land cover and OpenStreetMap are **not**
cached on disk. A second import of the same square downloads them again.

**Clear GIS caches** in **Editor Preferences → Plugins → TerrainForge GIS**
(or the console command `TerrainForge.GIS.ClearCaches`) deletes the tiles on
disk and the results in memory.

## Attribution and licences

Every import and level build records, for each dataset, **its source, its
licence and its attribution** in the definition's GIS provenance. An export
copies it into the export bundle. Use it to credit the data owners when you
ship.

- **Credit is required** for IGN, swisstopo, Kartverket, the Environment
  Agency, Copernicus DEM, ESA WorldCover, Terrain Tiles (the list in
  `Resources/GIS/terrain-attribution.txt`) and OpenStreetMap.
- **No credit is required** for USGS 3DEP (public domain), AHN (CC0) and
  Geobasis NRW (dl-de/zero-2.0). Giving it is good practice.
- **OpenStreetMap** data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open
  Database Licence (ODbL) 1.0. Masks and terrain made from it carry the credit
  "© OpenStreetMap contributors".

### The OpenStreetMap vectors are a database

An export can write the imported OpenStreetMap features as
`gis_vectors.geojson`. **That file is a database derived from OpenStreetMap.**
If you ship it, or a database made from it, you must offer it under the ODbL.

TerrainForge writes the notice and the attribution beside it, in a file with
the same name ending in `.ODbL.txt`. Keep the two together. If the notice
cannot be written, the export stops and says *"The vectors may only be shared
with it (ODbL)"*.

The masks are a different case: an image made from the data is a produced
work, which needs the credit, not the licence.

## Internet access

TerrainForge contacts these servers only when you use the map or the GIS
features:

| Data | Provider | Server |
|---|---|---|
| Elevation, France | IGN Géoplateforme | `data.geopf.fr` |
| Elevation, Switzerland | swisstopo | `data.geo.admin.ch` |
| Elevation, United States | USGS 3DEP | `elevation.nationalmap.gov` |
| Elevation, Norway | Kartverket | `wcs.geonorge.no` |
| Elevation, Netherlands | AHN (PDOK) | `service.pdok.nl` |
| Elevation, England | Environment Agency | `environment.data.gov.uk` |
| Elevation, North Rhine-Westphalia | Geobasis NRW | `www.wcs.nrw.de` |
| Elevation, worldwide | Copernicus DEM GLO-30 | `copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com` |
| Elevation, worldwide | Terrain Tiles | `s3.amazonaws.com` |
| Land cover, worldwide | ESA WorldCover | `esa-worldcover.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com` |
| OpenStreetMap features | Overpass API | `overpass-api.de`, or the servers you set |

The map adds its basemap's tile server and, when you press Enter in search,
the place-search service. **Requests carry the square's coordinates and
nothing else from your project**: no account, and no key except a map key you
entered for that provider.

Requests are polite by design:

- **At most four at a time to one server**, and one at a time to Overpass.
- A server that answers busy (429, 502, 503 or 504) is **waited for and
  retried**, honouring its own *Retry-After*.
- GIS requests go only to the servers above. A redirect to another host is refused:
  *"… redirected the request to another host."*

### When the public OpenStreetMap server is busy

The Overpass server at `overpass-api.de` is shared by everyone and is often
busy. When it gives up, TerrainForge says so in plain words:

> The public OpenStreetMap server (overpass-api.de) is busy; try again in a few
> minutes, or add another Overpass server in Editor Preferences > TerrainForge
> GIS.

Add servers under **Overpass endpoints** in **Editor Preferences → Plugins →
TerrainForge GIS**. They are tried in order. When one is busy and the next
answers, the import notes it; when all of them are busy, the import stops:

> OpenStreetMap (Overpass) is busy on every configured endpoint (…). Wait a
> minute and import again.

For heavy use, point it at an Overpass server of your own.
